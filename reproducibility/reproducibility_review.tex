\section{Within-date assignment dispersion}
For each of 34 dates and each split design, all $7!=5{,}040$ bijections between the seven requested routes and the seven frozen packets are enumerated using archived books. Shared access supplies one same-date seven-route reference. The assignment varies the route-to-packet mapping, not the partition of fields. No assignment is treated as an independent observation for inference.

Within each date, we compute the empirical 5th, 50th, and 95th percentiles of effective-book breadth and of its paired gap against shared access. Table~\ref{tab:assignment-dispersion} averages each date-level percentile across dates; it does not report pooled percentiles or confidence intervals. Mean breadth was checked against both frozen audited result objects, with maximum absolute discrepancy below $10^{-15}$.

The assignment-averaged mixed and coherent gaps are positive on every date, but individual assignments need not beat shared access. Mixed has \ADMixedBelowCount\ below-shared assignments over \ADMixedNegativeDates\ dates; coherent has \ADCoherentBelowCount\ over \ADCoherentNegativeDates\ dates, out of 171,360 assignments per design. There are no ties. The minimum paired gaps are $\ADMixedMinGap$ on \ADMixedWorstDate\ and $\ADCoherentMinGap$ on \ADCoherentWorstDate. The lowest date-level fractions above shared are \ADMixedMinAbovePercent\% and \ADCoherentMinAbovePercent\%, respectively. These descriptive tails qualify, rather than contradict, the positive assignment-averaged result.
